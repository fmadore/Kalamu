# 2026-11-03 · Live runs of Gemini, Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, economy mode and the newer models

## Context

Gemini, Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, economy mode and the models added from 26 October had been built
from the providers' documentation and tested only against recorded responses. On 3 November
2026 they ran against the live APIs with the keys saved in Kalamu's Settings, through
`cargo run -p kalamu --example live` on small samples: the French bulletin page
(`bulletin-page.jpg`), the 1915 front page of *The Monitor* (`the-monitor-1915.pdf`), the
bulletin's text, and a 52-second interview in French read by two of Windows' voices. Everything
together cost about $0.12.

The runner now prints, for each job, the estimate New job would show beside what each piece was
billed (tokens read, cached and written, pages, seconds) and takes `LIVE_PACE=economy`; plans
were added for Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, GPT-6.1 Terra, Mistral's chat models, GLM 5.3 Flash and
DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, and three texts under one analysis for what prompt caches save.

## Findings

- **Gemini 3.8 Flash** read both pages, transcribed the interview with its two speakers and
  answered the three analyses, for $0.022. The estimates were close for pages ($0.0092 against
  $0.0121) and recordings ($0.0018 against $0.0026), and five times too high for the short
  analyses ($0.0067 against $0.0013): the estimate assumes a longer answer than an entity list.
- **Gemini 3.5 Transcribe** answers as documented, one part per utterance with its `text`,
  and `audioTranscription` holding the same `text`, a `speakerLabel` and timed `words`
  (`"0.100s"`). Two things differ from Google's page: speakers are labelled `spk:0`, `spk:1`,
  counted from nought, not `spk_1`; and the usage reports tokens (1,307 of audio, 177 written for
  52 seconds, about 25 a second), not seconds. Kalamu showed the speakers as "Speaker spk:0" and
  priced the call at nothing, since its per-minute price met no seconds.
- **Economy mode works with all four providers.** Gemini, Claude and Mistral accepted their
  batches in the shapes Kalamu sends and answered in about 8, 7 and 1 minutes; the answers were
  read and billed at half price (Gemini's two pages $0.0061 against $0.0121 at once). Mistral
  Large 4.0, GLM 5.3 Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash went through batches as well, in about a
  minute; Mistral Medium 3.5's one request had not come back after 15 minutes. OpenAI
  accepted and validated both JSONL files but had answered none after 35 minutes, within its
  24-hour window.
- **Caches inside batches.** Claude's ordinary five-minute cache is read inside a batch: the
  instructions were written once (1,447 and 1,100 tokens) and read by the batch's other requests.
  Mistral reported 450 cached tokens on two of three analyses without a `prompt_cache_key`.
  Gemini cached nothing: the prompts are under its minimum.
- **OpenAI wrote every page to its cache.** GPT-6.1 Terra reported 9,420 of a page's 9,423 input
  tokens as cache writes, billed at 1.25× the input price. From GPT-5.6 on, OpenAI's implicit
  caching places its breakpoint at the end of the request, so each page, read once, was written
  to the cache at a quarter above its price.
- **Mistral Large 4.0** wrote 3,051 tokens for a short entity list, at reasoning `low`, so it
  cost 1.7× its estimate ($0.0071 against $0.0042). **Mistral Medium 3.5** (no reasoning) listed
  titles ("imam", "commandant de cercle") among the people. GPT-6.1 Terra, GLM 5.3 Flash and
  DeepSeek V4.1 Flash answered cleanly; DeepSeek's `reasoning_effort` was accepted.
- **Files left in storage.** Mistral's inline batches store their requests as a file
  (`inline_requests_….jsonl`, 951 KB for two pages) beside the results; OpenAI's keep the
  uploaded requests and the answers. The files from these runs were deleted by hand.

## Decisions

- **Gemini 3.5 Transcribe is billed by its tokens.** The catalogue gives it Google's token
  prices ($2 read, $12 written per million) beside the per-minute figure: the estimate stays by
  the minute, and the cost of a finished call comes from the tokens counted, about $0.0054 a
  minute in this run. The catalogue's date moves to 3 November so installed copies take it.
- **`spk:N` reads as Speaker N+1**, as Gemini's chat models number speakers; the documented
  `spk_N` keeps its number. The recorded test now uses the live answer's shape.
- **OpenAI caching becomes explicit.** Requests carry `prompt_cache_options.mode: "explicit"`
  and the instructions move from `instructions` into a developer message whose text block holds
  the only `prompt_cache_breakpoint`. A live check with 1,634 tokens of instructions wrote exactly
  those on the first call and read them on the second; with Terra's pages nothing was written,
  and the same two pages cost $0.0221 instead of $0.0250. Short instructions, under OpenAI's
  1,024-token minimum, are not cached in either mode. The pages no longer sit in OpenAI's cache.
- **Claude's one-hour cache in batches: not adopted.** The five-minute cache already served
  every request of these batches after the first, at 1.25× rather than 2× for the write. A
  batch large enough to run past five minutes between requests may still gain from it; that
  needs a run of a few hundred pages.

## Consequences

- Deleting the files a batch leaves in OpenAI's and Mistral's storage, once the answers are
  collected, stays on the roadmap; Mistral's inline batches need it as much as file batches.
- The analysis estimate overstates short answers and understates reasoning models such as
  Mistral Large 4.0; both stay on the side of caution or within twice the estimate.
- Not run here: offline Whisper, GLM 5.3 through a whole job, Terra on handwriting.
