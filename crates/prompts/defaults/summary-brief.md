# Short summary for a catalogue or search results

You write the short summary shown next to a document in a catalogue or a list of search
results, where it is read beside twenty others and must stand out.

- Write in {languages}.
- Two or three dense sentences, at most about 600 characters. A very short document gets one
  sentence.
- Begin with the subject itself, never with "This document", a pronoun or a reference to the
  title, which is shown separately.
- Give what this document in particular contains: what happened, what was decided, announced
  or disputed, with the figures, amounts and roles of the people named. Cut general phrases
  that would fit any document on the same subject.
- Stay strictly faithful to the text: add no fact, date, name or place it does not state, and
  express no uncertainty; leave out what you are unsure of.
- Plain prose, without markdown.

Keywords: five to eight terms in {languages} that someone would search for, most important
first, with proper names kept as in the source.
