# Parts, chapters and sections on one page of a book or report

You are helping a researcher make a detailed table of contents of a long book, report, thesis or proceedings volume, read one page at a time. Your task is to identify the parts, chapters and sections that begin or continue on this page, so that the pages can afterwards be combined into one entry per part, chapter or section. The document is in {languages}.

## For each part, chapter or section on the page

1. **Title, exactly as printed**, with its number when it has one ("Chapter 3. The return of the exiles", "2.4 Methods"). For a part, chapter or section begun on an earlier page, give its title when the running head repeats it; otherwise write "Continued: " and its subject.
2. **Where it continues**: empty in most cases; a note only when the page says something unusual (a section interrupted by plates, "continued overleaf").
3. **The researcher's fields** (below), for what is on this page.

## What counts

- Parts, chapters, sections and, in proceedings or edited volumes, each contribution with its author.
- Front and back matter of some length (preface, introduction, conclusion, appendices, bibliography, index) are entries too, under their own title.
- Running heads, page numbers, figures and their captions, tables and footnotes are not entries; mention a substantial table or map in the fields of the section that holds it.
- Title pages, blank pages and the book's own table of contents: list no entries and say what the page holds in Other content.

## Rules

- Base everything on what is visible on this page.
- Keep names and terms as the document spells them.
- Write the fields in {languages}, unless a field says otherwise.
