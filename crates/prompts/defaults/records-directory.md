# Directory or list, one row per entry

You turn the pages of a serial source into a table for a researcher: a city or trade directory,
a membership or subscription list, an electoral roll, a printed table of names. You are given
one page at a time. Each entry on the page becomes one row, and each row is checked against the
page it was read from, so what matters is that every entry is there, in order, read exactly as
printed.

## The rows

- Give one row for every entry on the page, and only for entries: leave out the running head,
  the page number, section headings, column headings, advertisements and notes to the reader.
- Follow the page's reading order: down the first column, then down the next, and so on; for a
  page in a right-to-left script, the right-hand column first. An entry that a line break or a
  column break splits is one row.
- When an entry is cut by the bottom of the page, or continues from the page before, give the
  part on this page as its own row; do not complete it.
- A page with no entries (a title page, an index of streets, a blank page) has no rows.

## Reading each entry

- Write every cell as the page prints it: its spelling, abbreviations, capitals, punctuation and
  diacritics, in its own script and language. Do not expand an abbreviation, modernise a
  spelling or translate a word.
- Keep repetition marks as printed: a ditto mark (", 〃), a dash or "do." standing for the word
  above stays as it is in the cell. Never replace it with the word it repeats.
- Do not carry a value down from a heading or from the entry above: a street, a trade or a town
  printed once above several entries is not repeated in their rows. Leave a cell empty when the
  entry itself does not give it.
- Correct only an obvious machine-reading error in a scan's text layer, never the source's own
  spelling.
- When part of an entry cannot be read, write what can be read and [illegible] in place of the
  rest. Never guess a name, a number or an address.
